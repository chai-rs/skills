# Register: voice, deixis, particles

One register — scaler peer-explainer. Migrated from CONTENT-GUIDE §4 + kien-thai's person
deixis discipline (MIT).

### `house-voice` *(register · scaler-explainer · hard)*

รุ่นพี่ที่เคยผ่านมาแล้ว เล่าให้ฟังตรง. An experienced engineer talking to a teammate with
2-3 years in — not a lecturer, not an influencer.

- Direct, not roundabout — say what it is
- Confident, not boastful — knows the material, doesn't show off
- Gives the reader credit — doesn't re-teach what they know, doesn't dumb down
- Honest about limits — says plainly what's hard, what has no fixed answer

Banned: hype (`ช็อก`, `เปลี่ยนโลก`, `สำคัญกว่าเดิมหลายเท่า`), motivational-speaker energy,
selling (`คอร์สนี้จะสอนคุณ...` — let the content speak).

### `pronouns` *(register · scaler-explainer · hard)*

- `คุณ` when addressing the reader directly (Thai often drops it — fine)
- `เรา` when walking through something together (`เราจะลองออกแบบระบบนี้กัน`)
- Never `ท่าน`, `ผู้เรียน`, `นักพัฒนา`, `ผม`, `ดิฉัน`, `แอด`

**Deixis continuity** (from kien-thai): once a 2nd-person frame is set — including the
implicit-reader frame with no pronoun named — hold it. Don't swap the reader's demographic
noun (`ผู้ใช้`, `เจ้าของระบบ`) in for `คุณ` mid-passage.

### `particle-frequency` *(register · scaler-explainer · soft)*

ครับ/ค่ะ/นะ/เลย are fine in moderation — they make the voice human. But never end **every**
sentence with ครับ (that is lecturer/presenter tone, not peer). ~1 per 4-5 sentences.

This is a judgment call, **not** a linter check — politeness is baseline Thai, not an AI-tell.
The linter never flags ครับ/ค่ะ; only this register note adjudicates cadence.

### `no-slang` *(register · scaler-explainer · hard)*

No internet slang or sound-spelling: `555`, `โคตร`, `ปังมาก`, `จัดไป`. That is influencer
tone, which the brand voice bans. These are in `forbidden-phrases.md` (linter error tier).
